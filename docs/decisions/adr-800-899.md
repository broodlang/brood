# Design decisions: numbers 800 to 899

One of the files the ADR log is split across by number; the index, and the notes that
apply to every entry, are in [../decisions.md](../decisions.md).

## ADR-800 — Each light's shadow map at its own size: two map arrays and a summed limit

**Status:** implemented (2026-11-25).

**Context.** ADR-788 keeps a scene's shadow maps on the GPU as one `texture_depth_2d_array`,
a layer a view, every layer as big as the largest view (`ShadowPlan::layer_side`): a smaller
view renders into the top-left of its layer and every read past its edge finds the layer
cleared to the far plane. The limit `MAX_SHADOW_TEXELS` (2²⁵) was checked the same way, views
× `layer_side`², in `mesh::shadow_plan` and in the spec parser. So every map was counted, and
allocated, at the largest one's size: a 4096 key spot with a point light's six cube maps at
1024 needed 7 × 4096² ≈ 117 M texels and was refused, though its maps are 23 M. The game of
ADR-786/793 asked for exactly that: a sharper edge on its figure's key shadow, 2.5 mm a texel
at 2048, 1.25 mm at 4096, beside the point light it already has.

**Decision.** Two map arrays instead of one: two size classes, not a full atlas.

- **Array 0** is at the largest view resolution in the plan and holds every view of that
  resolution; **array 1** is at the largest resolution among the others and holds every smaller
  view (absent when there are none). Within an array a view keeps its own `resolution`² corner
  of a layer, as before. `mesh::ShadowArrays` works the two out from the views' resolutions.
- **Each view knows its map** (`ShadowView::map`, a `ShadowMapSlot`: its array, its layer in
  it, and that array's side), set by `shadow_plan` after it has chosen the views. Every read
  that used the one layer side now uses the view's own map side: a read is clamped at the left
  and top to 0 … side − 1, and past the view's resolution up to that side it reads the cleared
  layer (far, lit) — the old semantics with `layer_side` replaced by the view's map side. Both
  painters do it: on the CPU `stored_depth` (and so `sample_shadow`, `bilinear_tap`,
  `soft_shadow`, `hair_shadow`) reads `view.map.side`; the ambient occlusion reads only inside
  its view and needed nothing.
- **The limit is the sum**: (views in array 0) × large² + (views in array 1) × small² ≤
  `MAX_SHADOW_TEXELS`, which stays 2²⁵. `shadow_plan` skips a light whose views would push the
  sum over (a smaller light after it may still fit); the parser refuses the scene, saying the
  sum: "the shadow maps need N texels together (M maps of …² and K maps of …²), more than
  33554432 …: lower a :resolution".
- **GPU.** Array 0 stays at binding 2 of the scene group; array 1 is binding 5, bound to the
  1-layer cleared fallback (`no_shadows`) when it is empty. The shadow passes' scene layout
  gets neither. `struct ShadowView` grows a `map: vec4<f32>` (its array's side, the array, the
  layer, spare), so `SceneU` is 3744 bytes and `SCENE_BYTES` 3840. The WGSL reads through
  `map_gather` and `map_load`, which branch on the view's array (comparison gathers and loads
  have no implicit derivatives, so the branch may be non-uniform). `params.z`, the old layer
  side, is spare. Each view renders into the layer of the array its `map` names.

Why two classes and not an atlas: it is the least change to every shadow read in both
painters (each read keeps its clamp-at-the-left, cleared-past-the-right rule, with a side per
view instead of one), and it covers the case asked for — one big map and smaller others. Biases,
filters, `:softness`, the soft shadows' blocker search, the hair shadow and the camera's
occlusion view (in whichever array its resolution puts it) are unchanged.

**Consequences.**

- The case asked for keeps 4096² + 6 × 1024² ≈ 23 M texels (about 92 MB of `Depth32Float`)
  where one array would have needed 7 × 4096² ≈ 117 M, over the limit.
- A third light at a third size pads to the small side (two spots at 4096 and 2048 and a cube
  at 512 keep the cube's six maps in 2048² layers).
- Next steps if they are wanted: a true atlas (every map packed at its own size), or a spot's
  map fitted to what it lights rather than to its whole cone.

## ADR-801 — A scene's passes timed on the GPU: timestamp queries read back a few frames late

**Status:** implemented (2026-11-25).

**Context.** The game of ADR-786 weighs each renderer change by its cost: 8× multisampling
(ADR-799) and the per-light shadow maps (ADR-800) were built, and their cost on the card could
not be told apart from the frame's other work. The paint's phases (`gui/latency`, ADR-440) are
the CPU's: recording and submitting passes, not running them. A film's frame time includes the
read-back. The hair work to come (scattered light, a deep opacity map, a blended pass) is to be
chosen by what each costs on the card, so the card's own time is needed.

**Decision.**

- **The device asks for `Features::TIMESTAMP_QUERY` where the adapter has it.** Nothing else
  changes on an adapter without it; `BROOD_GUI_GPU_TIMES=0` declines the timer on one that has it.
- **The stamps go on the passes themselves** (`timestamp_writes`), so no inside-encoder or
  inside-pass feature is needed. For each scene there are two stamps round its shadow passes (the
  first pass writes the start, the last the end) and two round its colour pass (the end includes
  the multisample resolve). At most 4 scenes a frame are timed, in a 16-query set. Queries are
  written in order and only those written are resolved: a query never written is never read
  (Vulkan would wait on it).
- **Read back a few frames late, never waited on.** Three read-back buffers. A frame is stamped
  only when one is free. After its submit the buffer is mapped asynchronously. A later frame's
  start polls the device without blocking, reads any mapped buffer into a sample and frees it.
  The painter never stalls on the card.
- **One log for the process, the newest 64 frames.** It is `host::gui::scene_gpu_times`, read by
  `(gui/scene-gpu-times)` as maps of milliseconds, `{:shadow :scene :scenes}`, summed over the
  frame's scenes. `BROOD_GUI_TRACE=1` prints the newest as `[gui-scene-gpu] shadow=…us
  scene=…us` beside each paint line. Not one log a window: a program draws its scenes in one
  window, and the films and probes that read this open one. The CPU painter records nothing.

**Consequences.**

- Each measured change gets a number on the card: `gui/scene-gpu-times` before and after, in the
  same program and view.
- The figures are the card's time on those passes, not the frame's: text, effects and the
  present are outside them, as is the time a pass waits for the one before.
- On a laptop GPU the clock can step between power states. The mean of many frames is the
  measure, not one frame.

## ADR-802 — A scene's hair in two passes: its coverage to depth, then its colour where that depth is its own

**Status:** implemented (2026-11-25).

**Context.** ADR-801's timestamps, split draw by draw, put the game's figure at 51 ms on the card
for its colour pass in a close view of her head (RTX 5060 laptop GPU, 900×1000, 4×), 89% of it in
four `:strands` meshes of wisps: 17.6, 14.4, 9.5 and 2.2 ms for 270k, 270k, 270k and 110k
triangles. Two trial switches (`BROOD_GUI_HAIR_TRIAL`, below) split that: drawn unlit, the
same hair took 1.7 ms; without its hair-on-hair shadow, 28 ms. So the cost was the lighting,
done for every layer of wisps over a pixel. A hair surface writes a sample mask (its coverage,
ADR-788/799), and a fragment shader that writes its mask or may `discard` loses the early depth
test: every layer was lit in full, then thrown away by the depth test behind the one in front.

**Decision.**

- **The hair's coverage goes to depth first.** At the start of a scene's colour pass, every
  `:hair` and `:strands` draw is drawn by `fs_hair_depth`: the same coverage (`hair_mask`, taken
  out of `fs_mesh`) as the sample mask, depth written, colour writes off. Nothing is lit.
- **Then its colour where that depth is its own.** In draw order as before, a hair draw uses
  `fs_hair_front` with the depth test EQUAL and depth writes off. Its shader writes no mask and
  has no `discard` (`mesh_lit`, the lighting tail of `fs_mesh`, has none), so the early depth
  test runs: a sample takes the colour of the hair the depth pass left in front, and the layers
  behind are never lit. The samples the coverage left uncovered keep what is behind, so the soft
  edges stay soft.
- **The vertex position is `@invariant`**, so the two passes put a sample at the same depth to
  the bit, which the equal test needs.
- **On by default**; `BROOD_GUI_HAIR_PREPASS=0` draws hair in one pass as before, to compare.
- **`BROOD_GUI_HAIR_TRIAL`** (an override constant, for measuring only): 1 draws hair by its
  coverage alone, lit by nothing; 2 without its hair-on-hair shadow.

**Consequences.**

- On that view: the colour pass 50.9 → 8.2 ms on the card (hair draws 43.7 → 2.6 ms, the depth
  pass about 1.9 ms). Hair's cost now follows the pixels it covers once, not the layers deep.
- Not byte-identical. Where two crossing strands lie at exactly the same depth at a sample, one
  pass kept the first drawn and the equal test lets the later one win: on that view 0.3% of
  pixels, scattered through the dense side lengths (PSNR 55.7 dB, mean difference 0.015 of a
  level). The brightness and edge energy of hair crops agree within 0.2%: no edge turned hard.
- The CPU painter is unchanged. Its hair parity test against the GPU passes in both modes.
- Every hair vertex is transformed twice (the strands' ribbons are built in the vertex stage):
  cheap against the shading saved, about 1.9 ms on 0.9 M wisp vertices.

## ADR-803 — Native code for functions holding a `(fn …)` literal

**Status:** implemented, default on (2026-11-25); `BROOD_NO_JIT_CLOSURES=1` opts out.

**Context.** A chunk holding a `MakeClosure` was outside the JIT subset, so any function that
builds a closure ran on the VM for good, with all of its own float and vector work. b3d-skeleton
had 38 such functions, among them the world-matrix chain of a joint run (about a fifth of the
game process's tick). The 2026-08-29 attempt (`docs/compute-frontier.md`, "Scoped 2026-08-29")
lowered the literal behind `BROOD_MKCLO=1`, found the winners still failing the call-mediated
gate, removed that gate outright and lost every row; the opt-in lowering was later deleted.

**Decision.**

- **The literal lowers as one callback**, `brood_rt_make_closure(heap, out, inst)`, which runs
  the VM's own `MakeClosure` arm on the capture values the native code stages on `roots`; the
  `inst` pointer is baked into the code, pinned by the arm keep-alive like `brood_rt_const_load`'s
  constants. The emitter treats it as a safepoint: handles live beneath the captures are spilled
  to the reserved slots first (`jit_spill_reserve` already counted `MakeClosure` as a producer)
  and the roots base is reloaded after. Errors park and leave by the error block.
- **The call-mediated gate is untouched.** A first version exempted every closure-building arm
  from it; measured, the exemption won nothing (nqueens' `solve` passes the gate already —
  `reduce` fuses into a self-loop — and needed only the literal admitted) and admitted pure
  plumbing (matmul's setup: +1.2% instructions). The gate's cost model stands.
- **A chunk of only call plumbing keeps the literal out** (`only_call_plumbing`: loads, stores,
  calls and literals, no branch, primitive, collection literal or loop). Native code for it runs
  the VM's own callbacks, so it pays a compile (two, with the hot re-lowering) for nothing:
  matmul's setup closure and the prelude's `map` wrapper cost +1.2% more until this rule.
  Narrowed to chunks holding a literal, so no arm that lowered before changes.
- **A chunk calling `%receive` keeps the literal out**: every receive builds its matcher as a
  literal, so those chunks stay exactly as they were, the ADR-410 head receive included.

**Why default on.** The 2026-08 boot cost (+11 ms a run for tiering the boot path's closure
arms) no longer shows: on the test machine, instructions per row (three runs each, std image
live), `BROOD_NO_JIT_CLOSURES=1` → default: `nqueens` −9.8% (1688 → ~1400 ms wall at
`BENCH_N=12`), `latency` −3.4%, `spawn-live` −1.9%, `startup` +0.1%, `matmul` +0.1%, every other
row within ±0.3%; `http` and `spawn`, which read +5.6% and +1.8% once, overlap completely over
six alternating five-run batches (noise).

**A native miss on a name a load would bind now loads it.** Lowering more closures put a call
in native code that the VM had always run: `image_registrations_match_source`'s registry walk
calls `seq/keep` inside `%isolate-discard-loads`, which rolls `seq`'s load back, so the next
round's native code found the name gone and raised `unbound symbol: seq/keep`, where the VM's
miss path (`global_miss`) loads the module again. Native code now does the same: a non-tail
call's miss loads synchronously in `jit_dispatch_call` (a safepoint, as the VM's nested run
does); a global read or a tail call's head, where no collection may run, DEOPTs
(`global-miss-loads`) when the name's module is unloaded or loading elsewhere, and the VM's
re-run takes its lazy path. Any other miss is the unbound error, as before. A fresh lazy name
is bound to its loading stub, so this is reached only after a binding the code was compiled
against has gone. Guard: `crates/cli/tests/jit_isolate_lazy_miss.rs` (tail and non-tail, red
with the fixes removed).

**Consequences.** `crates/cli/tests/jit_closure_literal.rs` holds five shapes (a handle beneath
the literal, a `letrec` self-name read as a value, a capture-free literal promoted from native
code, a literal returned as the value, many processes at once) native with answers equal to the
lever-off run, and a plumbing arm out; sabotaged red three ways (captures staged out of order,
the self-name left unbound, the plumbing rule off).

## ADR-804 — An eye's white reflects about the eyeball's own normal

**Status:** implemented (2026-11-25). Completes ADR-796's "not yet" for the white's highlights.

**Context.** ADR-796 left the line that misses the cornea's cap (the white) as its mesh has it,
and blended the cornea's normal into the mesh's at the limbus. The game's eye mesh was modelled
for a painted iris: around the limbus it leans as a bowl does. Under a mirror-smooth tear-film
`:coat`, that lean reflected a strip of the room beside the iris: a chrome band at the iris's
nasal edge and a bright band at its upper side. Measured across her white, the strip rose to 167
against the white's 125–133.

**Decision.** Where an eye frame is known, the white reflects about the eyeball sphere's own
normal at the fragment, `normalize(world − centre)`: its specular, `:coat` and reflections, and
the surface a reflection must not go under. The light still falls on the mesh's normal, so the
lids' shade, the ambient occlusion and the texture's shading stay the mesh's. At the limbus the
cornea's normal gives way to the ball's, not the mesh's. The same in both painters (`eye_seen`
and its callers).

**Consequences.** The band is gone: across her white the brightness stays at 125–131 and darkens
to 44–63 at the limbus, a soft dark ring as on a real eye. An eye mesh no longer has to be a good
sphere for its highlights to be one. A white modelled with deliberate bumps reflects as a smooth
ball (a normal map on the white would need to be laid over the ball's normal: not done).

## ADR-805 — Skin pressed by contacts: laid onto what presses it, soft flesh rising round it

**Status:** implemented (2026-11-25).

**Context.** The game of ADR-786 flattened its figure's skin with a shape key per case: breasts
on a hand, a bottom on a seat, thighs meeting, a cheek on glass. Each was made by hand, each was
one more key (91, against a limit of about 150), and a fingertip's soft imprint had none. Its
author asked for one deformation for all skin: "it must be universal on the body".

**Decision.**

- **`:contacts`** on a scene: at most 16 shapes in world space, `{:plane [nx ny nz d]}` (inside
  where n·p < d), `{:sphere [x y z r]}`, `{:capsule [ax ay az bx by bz r]}`. A material's
  **`:deform true`** marks the draws whose skin they press (not strands, hair or an eye).
- **What a pressed vertex does** (`mesh::deform_by_contacts`, the shader's twin): inside a
  contact, it is laid onto the contact's surface along the surface's normal, and its normal turns
  to face into the contact (out of the body). The patch's edge is eased over `EDGE_BAND` (4 mm)
  + `EDGE_SPREAD` (0.5) × the depth (the distance's smooth maximum with 0), so skin meets the
  surface tangentially and no crease marks where the patch ends. A firm part (softness under
  `FIRM_SOFTNESS`, 0.5) sits up to `FIRM_INSET` (a quarter) of the depth behind the surface, a
  nipple sunk into the softer breast round it. Just outside, flesh of softness *s* (a mesh's **`:softness`**, f32 0 bone … 1 soft
  a vertex) rises along its normal in a ring that starts at nothing at the patch's edge, crests a
  little way out and falls away (√2·r·e^(½−r²)), `RING_LIFT` (0.35) × *s* × the
  contact's deepest press at its crest, as wide as (`RING_WIDTH` (12 mm) + `RING_SPREAD` (2) × that
  depth) × (0.5 + *s*), so fat spreads wide and firm flesh barely rises, its normal tilted with the ring's slope. That is roughly the dent's volume spread
  round it. Each contact's deepest press is found over all the scene's pressed vertices together,
  so meshes that meet (a body and its face) rise alike at a seam.
- **`:drag [x y z]`** on a contact (metres, along its surface): the friction that holds skin to it.
  Pressed skin moves by it wholly inside the patch and less through its edge and ring, so the
  skin round it stretches with it. The game keeps the stick and slip (an anchor, the shear, a
  damped snap-back); brood draws the offset it is given.
- **At most `MAX_SQUASH` (2 cm) × softness** is skin laid toward a contact. Pushed further (a
  pose that drives a face deep through glass), skin passes the surface with the rest of her rather
  than being flattened away from what lies under it, which opened her eye and her nostrils.
- **`:skip [j …]`** on a contact (up to four joints, the mesh's skin joint indices): its own limb.
  A vertex whose weights on those joints pass a half is neither pressed by it nor sizes its ring:
  a thigh's capsule, lying inside that thigh, presses the other thigh only.
- **The ring and the eased edge are sized by the press as far as skin can squash** (the contact's
  deepest press capped at `MAX_SQUASH`), never by a raw depth: one part driven deep through a
  contact (her feet through the floor in a crouch) once made the floor's ring centimetres high and
  half a metre wide, swelling her thighs and bottom.
- **`:only [j …]`** on a contact (up to four joints): the one limb whose skin alone it presses, a
  vertex pressed only where its weights on them pass a half: a plane between her thighs pressing
  one thigh, its twin the other, her crotch's midline (on neither leg) untouched.
- **Softness 0 is never pressed**, and sizes no ring: bone, and the limbs that press (a hand's and
  forearm's own skin, which the capsules standing for them lie inside). A mesh without a
  `:softness` is soft throughout.
- **GPU: two compute passes before a scene's shadow passes**, only for a scene with contacts and
  pressed draws. Pass A poses each pressed vertex once (the vertex stage's own `pose`, reading
  the mesh's vertex buffer as storage) into the scene's posed buffer (world position, normal,
  tangent) and keeps each contact's deepest press by an atomic max. Pass B presses each posed
  vertex. The vertex stages (`vs_mesh`, `vs_shadow`) read a pressed draw's vertices from that
  buffer instead of skinning them again. `DrawU` gains `deform` (pressed, first posed vertex,
  has a softness, vertex count): 352 bytes.
- **CPU: the same after posing** (`press_posed`), the depths over all pressed draws first.

**Consequences.**

- One deformation for floors, mats, panes, walls, seats, fingers and forearms, and limbs
  meeting. The game passes the shapes it already knows. A shape key that models a mass's change
  (a breast settling, cupped) stays a key; one that only flattens can go, once a film shows the
  contact doing as well.
- Nothing runs and nothing is kept without `:contacts`: no pass, no buffer.
- A pressed draw is skinned once a frame, not once per shadow view and colour pass.
- A ring is a lift along the normal, not a simulation: it does not slide flesh along the
  surface, and two contacts close together add their rings.
- Bone is not pressed (softness 0): keeping a hand out of a shin stays the game's job.

## ADR-806 — Numeric lists cross a WASM component call by memory copy

**Status:** implemented (2026-11-25); `BROOD_NO_WASM_FAST_LISTS=1` opts out.

**Context.** `%wasm-call` marshals through wasmtime's dynamic API: every argument becomes a
`Val`, a `list<float64>` a `Vec<Val>` of one `Val` per element, each built, type-checked and
stored, and the result read back the same way. b3d measured ~45 ns a float on top of ~4.4 µs a
call, so its skeleton kernel in Rust (5 600 floats each way) ran slower as a component than
the Brood it replaced.

**Decision.**

- An export whose parameters are all numeric lists (`list<float64|float32|s32|u32>`, one to
  four of them) and whose result is one such list or nothing is called through wasmtime's
  typed API, whose primitive-list lowering and lifting copy the bytes in one pass
  (`host/wasm/lists.rs`). The shape is decided once per export at load.
- One typed signature per arity covers every mix of element types: `NumList` (in) and
  `NumListOut` (out) each stand for any of the four, implementing wasmtime's
  `ComponentType`/`Lower`/`Lift` by delegating to its own `[T]`/`Vec<T>` implementations for
  the element type the export declares (read from the `InterfaceType` at the call).
- Those trait items are hidden, reached through `wasmtime::component::__internal` (what
  wasmtime's derive macros expand to) and outside its stability guarantee. A wasmtime upgrade
  that changes them fails this file's build, not a run; the opt-out lever is the fallback.
- Answers and errors are the general path's: an int where a float is declared, the same range
  error for an s32/u32 that does not fit, a vector back.
- **A numeric array argument takes one shape at every native boundary** (`host/numbers.rs`):
  a vector or list of numbers, or of vectors/lists of numbers, flattened one level. b3d found
  that packing its 176-joint pose into one flat list in Brood cost ~510 µs, more than its
  whole Brood pass, so a free crossing bought nothing; its matrices now go in as they are. The
  general path takes the same shape for the same four element types, so the two paths still
  answer alike.

**Consequences.** Measured on the test machine with a WAT kernel that doubles each element:
5 600 floats in and out, 439 → 32 µs a call (13.7×, ~2.7 ns a float both ways); one float,
1.7 → 1.5 µs; b3d's pose as 176 sixteen-float vectors in, 2 816 floats out, 23 µs. Guards:
`tests/wasm_lists_test.blsp` (every element type, a mix, no result, nesting, the errors) and
`crates/cli/tests/wasm_fast_lists.rs`, which runs the same calls both ways, requires every
answer and error to match, and requires the copy to be 3× faster (it is 13×) — the one check
that tells the two paths apart.

## ADR-807 — Native numeric kernels through one narrow C ABI

**Status:** implemented (2026-11-25), feature `native-kernels`, default on.
Amends docs/interop.md ("The one native exception").

**Context.** docs/interop.md rejects a native plugin ABI: tied to the runtime's layout, able
to crash it, a supply-chain hole. Those arguments are about native code that sees Brood values
and ships in packages. b3d's hot numeric pass needs neither: arrays of doubles in and out, in
the project's own code. With ADR-806 its WASM component is fast, but a native build is
faster still and is what a desktop game runs.

**Decision.** `std/native.blsp` over four primitives (`%native-open`, `%native-call`,
`%native-close`, `%native-forget`, `host/native.rs`):

- **One signature:** `int32_t name(const double *in, size_t in_count, double *out, size_t
  out_count)`. No Brood value crosses, so the library needs nothing of `Value`, the heap or
  the GC. A nonzero return is raised as an error naming the kernel and the code. The input is
  ADR-806's numeric array shape (nested vectors flatten one level); the output is a vector of
  floats. No int-array variant until a kernel needs one (ADR-011).
- **Never from a fetched package:** a path under a `_deps` directory is refused in the
  mechanism, whatever the policy asked. A package ships native code as a WASM component.
- **The grammar defences (ADR-660), minus vetting:** every call (and the load, which runs the
  library's initialisers) is bracketed by a `ForeignCode` mark, so a fatal signal's record names
  the library and writes its quarantine, keyed by its SHA-256 under
  `$XDG_STATE_HOME/brood/kernel-verdicts`; `open` refuses quarantined bytes and says why;
  `native/forget` clears a verdict, and a rebuilt library is new bytes. Vetting in a child is
  left out: it needs a corpus, and only the package knows a kernel's valid inputs.
- **Like a WASM instance otherwise:** an unguessable int token owned by the opening process
  and closed when it exits; `%native-call` is offload-safe, so `native/call-blocking` runs a
  long kernel on the ADR-144 pool.
- **Named `native`, not `kernel`:** in this repo "the kernel" is the Rust runtime.

**Consequences.** Measured on the test machine with a C kernel that doubles each element:
5 600 doubles each way 19.7 µs a call, one double 0.35 µs; b3d's pose as 176 sixteen-float
vectors, 14 µs. Most of the remaining time is converting Brood vectors to and from doubles.
`tests/native_kernel_test.blsp` compiles a C library with `cc` (skipped without one) and
covers calls, nesting, error codes, a missing entry point, the offloaded call, the `_deps`
refusal and a crash in a child runtime that quarantines the library until `forget`; red with
the `_deps` check and the quarantine check each removed. The `brood` binary's lean
`--no-default-features` bundle leaves the feature out, so a shipped app falls back to its
component or its Brood version.

## ADR-808 — Three per-call costs every program paid: float `max`/`min`, shims that check nothing, `contains?` on a set

**Status:** implemented (2026-11-25).

**Context.** womanblend's frame: `math/clamp` ~1 ms a tick (~400 ns a call), `contains?` on
sets ~0.9 ms (~200 ns a call). Measured apart, three separate costs, none specific to the game.

**Decision.**

1. **A float `max`/`min` lowers** (`min_max`, jit_lower/emit.rs). It had no float lowering, so
   `emit_float_arith`'s fallback bailed the WHOLE arm (under the mislabelled reason
   `float-eq-mixed-int-float-unsafe`): `math/clamp` and everything that inlined it ran on the
   VM. The lowering is the VM's `extremum` exactly: the first argument stands unless
   `first < second` (max) / `first > second` (min) as a numeric ordering, so a tie and a NaN
   keep the first and the answer is the winning OPERAND (an int that wins stays an int). Two
   certain floats compare and select unboxed; otherwise the operands' words are compared by tag
   (int × int as ints, an int/float mix as floats) and the winner's words are the answer; a
   non-number deopts. Taken when an operand is a float, in a float-context arm, against a
   literal int bound, and on the polymorphic-dispatch path; two certain ints keep the int path.
2. **A contract that can refuse nothing binds no shim** (`std/contract.blsp` `shim`). Every type
   in `math/clamp`'s `(x:?A lo:?A hi:?A -> ?A)` is a type variable, which accepts any value
   (`symbol_verdict`), and `math/max`'s is `(& ?A -> ?A)`: their shims vouched for every call
   and still cost ~950 ns, and hid the primitive from the compiler, which inlines `%max` only
   when it sees it. Under `nest run`/`nest test` (contracts armed), a clamp step 980 → 35 ns,
   the unarmed figure. A declaration made only of type variables and `any` keeps the original.
3. **`%set-has?` is a primitive op** (`PrimOp::SetHas`, `Heap::set_has_inline`, shared by the
   VM's `Prim2` arm and `brood_rt_set_has`), and `contains?` is `(if (set? m) (%set-has? m k)
   (%contains-key? m k))` with the map branch moved to `%contains-key?` in tail position. It
   held the map branch inline, so it carried non-tail calls and the profitability gate kept it
   on the VM. From a native caller a membership test is ~56 ns over the loop, from ~890.

Found on the way: `math/rem` armed costs ~300 ns against 15 unarmed. Its `(a:int b:int -> int)`
is a real check; the dispatcher's cost for it is its own question, not addressed here.

**Consequences.** Guards: `tests/jit_min_max_float_test.blsp` (every edge of `extremum`, and
`math/clamp`'s own arm settling native under the suite's armed contracts, which needs both 1
and 2; red with `min_max` disabled on both its routes), `tests/contains_native_test.blsp`
(`contains?` settles native; every answer and error it had), and `%set-has?` in
`resolve_prim`'s table test (the restructure alone makes `contains?` native, so only the table
pins the inlined probe).

## ADR-809 — Guards against today's failure classes: both contract modes, exhaustive dispatch, an eager-tier suite

**Status:** implemented (2026-11-25).

**Context.** One day's work (ADR-803..808) hit the same few failure classes repeatedly: guards
that passed with their fix removed (the suite arms contracts, which turned `math/max` into a
shim, so the JIT op under test never ran); catch-all arms that hid unhandled cases (a float
`max`/`min` fell into `_ => bail` under another op's reason; the `empty?` fallback still treated

**Decision.**

- **Both contract modes, locally.** `make test-file TEST=…` runs files armed and unarmed, the

- **Exhaustive where a new case must be decided.** `emit_float_arith` matches every `PrimOp`

- **Refactors for the next change of the same kind:** heap probes in the JIT come from one

## ADR-810 — What b3d's kernels left on the Brood side: records by key, results in rows, a cheaper `conj`; 64 contacts

**Status:** implemented (2026-11-26).

**Context.** With ADR-806/807 the kernels themselves were fast (b3d's skeleton 28 µs against
272 µs in Brood), but the Brood around them was not: her pose is 176 maps `{:translation [3]
:rotation [4] :scale [3]}`, and gathering it into the shape the boundary took cost ~260 µs, and
splitting the 2 816 floats back into matrices ~100 µs. Underneath both, `(conj v x)` cost ~300 ns
an element. Separately, ADR-805's 16 contacts dropped 30 of womanblend's 46 shapes.

**Decision.**

- **Records by key** (`host/numbers.rs`): a numeric-array argument may be `{:records rs :keys
  ks}` — each record (a map or a `defrecord` value) contributes its values under `ks` in order,
  each a number or a vector of numbers. For `native/call` and the WASM numeric lists alike. A
  record missing a key is refused, naming its position: a kernel fed a short array reads garbage.
- **Results in rows:** `native/call`'s count may be `[count size]`, answering `count` vectors of
  `size` floats; `wasm/call-shaped inst name size & args` does the same for a list result. Built
  natively, with no split in Brood.
- **`conj` has a fixed arm:** `((coll x) …)` beside `((coll & xs) …)`, so the call nearly every
  `conj` is links without a rest list, and appends with the new kernel op `%vector-push` (one
  item, no carrying collection) — the multi-arity lever CLAUDE.md's `+` example prescribes.
- **A builtin in tail position is called directly** from a native caller's outcome-4 path
  (`finish_nested`), as a non-tail builtin call already was, not through `apply_value`.
- **A small append builds its inline store in place** (`vector_append`), not through a `Vec`
  malloc'd, filled and freed.
- **A closure literal reserves its spill slots** (`jit_spill_reserve`, `operands_below_safepoints`):
  a literal is a safepoint as a non-tail call is, but the reserve counted only calls, so an arm
  whose only safepoint was a literal — `(pick (get m :k) (fn …))` in tail position — got none and
  bailed `mkclo-spill-exhausted` (24 of womanblend's arms). Guard: `jit_closure_spill_test`.
- **64 contacts** (`MAX_CONTACTS`): the room's 4 and two arms' 42 fit. Both painters loop over
  the scene's own count, so the cost is per contact used, never per slot; the shader's three
  literal sizes are held to the constant by `the_mesh_shader_validates`.

**Consequences.** b3d's pose by keys with rows back: 25 µs a call against 334 µs gathering and
splitting in Brood. A `conj` loop: 300 → ~100 ns an element (16 elements: 300 → 131). Still
open, and the next lever for `conj`: a native tail call to a builtin still leaves its arm (the
outcome-4 exit); lowering it as a direct call and return would take the rest. Per-draw contact
culling is not done: womanblend's whole body is one draw, so it would need per-vertex-patch
culling to help; 64 makes it unneeded for now.

## ADR-811 — The rest of the native-code gaps from womanblend's run: `count` of a record, float `=`, contracted int ops, a builtin in tail position

**Status:** implemented (2026-11-26).

**Context.** After ADR-810, womanblend's scripted run still showed: `head-held` latched off
native code on `count-declined`; three arms bailing `float-eq-mixed-int-float-unsafe` (genuine
float `=`); `math/rem` costing ~300 ns a call with contracts armed against 15 unarmed; and
`conj`'s remaining cost, a tail call to a builtin that left native code (outcome 4) for its
caller's general dispatch.

**Decision.**

- **`count` re-tiers as a call.** The inline answers what the heap can size and declines a
  receiver only Brood can count (a record's `Counted`/`Seqable`, a lazy view); native code cannot
  run Brood at a primitive, so each decline deopted until the thrash latch. After four, deopt
  feedback flags the arm (`jit_count_calls`) and its next lowering calls the real `count` there:
  the head's binding read uncached and called as an ordinary computed call, a safepoint. Guard:
  `jit_count_call_test`.
- **Float `=`.** Brood's `=` on two floats is IEEE equality (`0.0` = `-0.0`, NaN equals
  nothing) and an int never equals a float. Two certain floats compare with one ordered `fcmp`;
  a float beside anything else goes to the structural `eq_dispatch`, never to the float path
  that bailed the arm. Guard: `jit_float_equal_test`.
- **Contracted int ops inline.** `resolve_prim` looks through a contract shim declared exactly
  `(int int -> int)` over an op whose inline answers only two ints (`%rem`, `%quot`, the bit
  ops), as `sqrt`'s look-through does: every other operand defers to the live head, the shim,
  which checks. Not `+ - * /`: their float path would answer a float the shim refuses. Armed
  `math/rem` 301 → 14 ns. Guard: `int_op_inline_looks_through_a_contract_shim_it_cannot_violate`.
- **A builtin in tail position is called in place.** The tail path tests the staged callee's
  tag (`TAG_NATIVE`, pinned by the layout test) and, for a builtin, calls it
  (`brood_rt_tail_native`) and returns its value as the arm's own; any other callee exits as
  before, for one compare. Like a non-tail builtin call from native code, a builtin that parks
  then blocks its worker once and the suspend-host latch keeps that arm on the VM — correct, and
  the same exposure the non-tail path has always had.

**Consequences.** The `count` re-tier and float `=` take the last two latch/bail classes the run
showed. A `conj` loop's call overhead is gone from the profile; what is left of its ~100 ns is
the allocation and collection of a fresh vector a step, which immutability makes inherent.
## ADR-812 — Soft flesh lagging its bones: a draw's `:loose` palette

**Status:** accepted, 2026-11-26.

**Context.** womanblend's user: her flesh is "too stiff, it flips back too quickly, so it's more a
vibration", and "i need some thigh jiggle as well". Her breasts and glutes are spring joints
(b3d-springs), heavy masses with their own bones; the rest of her soft flesh (thighs, belly, the
backs of her arms) moved rigidly with its bones. A per-vertex simulation kept in the renderer would
need state between frames: wrong for a film drawn only at chosen moments, for the CPU painter, and
for frames drawn two behind in her visual workers.

**Decision.** A draw may carry `:loose {:palette j :amount a}` (a skinned `:deform` draw: both
painters pose its skin once a frame already). Palette j is the same skeleton's, each joint where a
damped follower of it is this frame, kept and stepped by the game on its own clock (b3d's
`b3d-loose`: a follower answers its point's acceleration, damped on its speed relative to the
point's, held within a reach). A vertex of softness s (the mesh's `:softness`, ADR-805; none: 1) is
placed s × a (held to 0 … 1) of the way from where the draw's own palette puts it to where palette j
does; its normal stays the draw's own (the trail is a centimetre). The CPU painter does it in
`pose_vertices`, the GPU in the contact passes' posing (`cs_contact_depth`, `loose_world`), so a
loose draw is posed through those passes even with no contacts (`DrawU::loose`: amount, the
palette's offset and joint count; `DrawU` 368 bytes). The renderer keeps no state.

**Consequences.** Bone stays on its bones, soft flesh trails and settles, alike on both painters
(`loose_flesh_follows_its_lagging_bones_alike_on_both_painters`) and in any film. The lag of a
joint is rigid (its frame moved by the trail), so the wobble's shape is the softness map's; finer
patterns would need more followers or per-vertex state. The cost is a second skinning in the posing
pass for loose draws, and on the game's side a few followers and sixteen numbers a followed joint.

## ADR-813 — Hair at its real width: the `:fibres` surface, its light and its contacts

**Status:** accepted, 2026-11-26.

**Context.** womanblend's hair as camera-facing ribbons (`:strands`) or alpha cards (`:hair`)
read as cards or as fuzz at every width tried (her `docs/research/hair-lessons.md`); the look the
user chose (her E1 lock: thousands of real strands) needs a strand drawn at its real width, a tenth
of a millimetre, far under a pixel, without aliasing into dashes or washing out into a haze. Then
the whole head: tens of thousands of strands against her skin, the glass she presses and the
room's light from every side.

**Decision.** A material `:surface :fibres` draws a strand mesh (two vertices a point, its normal
its direction, as `:strands`) at its real width:

- **Coverage, exactly (TressFX's ShortCut).** Each fibre is drawn 0.7 of a pixel wider each side
  than it is and its fragment's coverage of its pixel worked out from its real width; the scene's
  colour pass draws everything else, then the fibre passes keep each pixel's three nearest fibre
  depths, light those three, and blend the rest as one mean colour by the pixel's transmittance
  (`fibres.rs`). No alpha hash, no mask; a strand a tenth of a pixel wide covers a tenth.
- **Lit as Karis 2016 (UE4's hair).** R, TT and TRT, and his stand-in for light scattered through
  many fibres; each strand a little rougher, tilted and darker than the next by the mesh's
  `:strand` value (1 f32 a vertex, constant along a strand).
- **Its own shadow, baked.** The mesh's `:visibility` (4 f32 a vertex, `[a bx by bz]`: the hairs
  crossed toward a light L are max(0, a + b·L)) tints and dims each light by the hair it passed
  through, `:density` (0.4) how much; the shadow maps' estimate where a mesh has none.
- **A light that knows what lies between.** Light THROUGH a strand (TT, and the scatter's part
  from the strand's far side) comes only from a light with a shadow map: a shadowless light
  (womanblend's fill, rim, leg light) lights hair by reflection alone. Without this, her
  shadowless fill, behind her head seen from behind, lit the back of her hair near white through
  her head at any colour, and no density could help (the bake counts hair, not her head).
- **Laid on glass, never through.** `:clamp-planes [[px py pz nx ny nz] …]` (at most 4, on
  `:strands` and `:fibres`): a strand point within `CLAMP_BAND` (8 mm) of a plane is eased toward
  its face, `HOLD + (BAND − HOLD)·e^((d − BAND)/(BAND − HOLD))` from it (unchanged at the band's
  edge with the same slope, never nearer than `CLAMP_HOLD`, 0.5 mm, however deep), its direction
  bent by that easing's slope so it lies along the glass. Both painters and the shadow pass alike.

The CPU painter draws `:fibres` as `:strands` (its ribbons, clamped alike); the fibre passes are
GPU-only, made only for a scene that draws `:fibres` (a scene without hair pays nothing).

**Amended 2026-11-26 (c).** A `:fibres` mesh casts its shadow from one strand in `FIBRE_SHADOW_SHARE`
(8), each drawn that many times as wide, so the shadow covers as much: its triangles are ordered at
upload by each strand's own random number (`:strand`), so the shadow pass draws a leading share, an
even random choice of whole strands. The shadow pass had been most of a head's cost (10–20 ms
against 0.5 bald, 39). And the device asks for the card's own buffer limits: at wgpu's default
256 MB a 49 152-strand mesh (3.1 M vertices) was dropped at upload.

**Amended 2026-11-26 (b).** The three nearest fibres are composited front to back, not averaged:
each stores its coverage with its depth (the top 24 bits order them, the low 8 hold it), and the
front pass weighs a fibre's colour by its coverage times what the nearer of the three let
through. Averaged alike, the front strand counted no more than those behind it and the hair
read over-blended (the user). The hair's opacity is unchanged: still exact over every fibre.

**Amended 2026-11-26.** The baked self-shadow tints the light crossing the hair by each fibre's own
transmittance, from its colour by Chiang & Bitterli 2016's absorption at its roughness (exp(−2σa)
a crossing), not a fixed ginger fibre: by the lamp and from behind every colour had read the same
orange.

**Consequences.** Real-width hair without aliasing or haze, its own shadow without a per-frame
shadow pass of its own, its back no brighter than what lights it, pressed flat on glass. The bake
is the asset's (Blender), redone when the hair's shape changes. Three layers a pixel are lit; the
rest are averaged, which a thick dark mass hides and a thin light one could show.

**Amended 2026-11-26 (d).** A strand blocks only part of the light it crosses: each `:fibres`
shadow fragment is kept with probability its material's `:shadow-keep` (0.15 without one), drawn by strand and texel
(stochastic transparent shadows, Enderton et al. 2010), so the shadow filter averages a thin layer
of hair into a faint shade and only overlapping layers into a strong one. Whole, a strand drawn
`FIBRE_SHADOW_SHARE` times wide stamped full shadow: dark blotches on her forehead under thin hair
at the temple. The user chose 0.2 from six strengths side by side, then 0.15 seen live; as a material
key (the user: "cant this be a setting?") the game sets it with no rebuild.

## ADR-814 — Skinning that blends rotations: each vertex turning about its centre of rotation

**Status:** accepted, 2026-11-26.

**Context.** womanblend's armpit at full reach is carried by four joints with 150° between them;
linear blend skinning (the blended matrices) collapses it to a third of its size across and turns
faces inside out: the user's jagged armpits, the ticks under her collarbones with folded arms, the
"beak" of her bottom in a low squat. No shape key can cure it. Dual quaternions bulge. Le & Hodgins
2016 ("Real-time skeletal skinning with optimized centers of rotation", Disney Research) keep a
joint's cross-section rigid for the cost of about one extra skinning a vertex.

**Decision.** A mesh may carry `:centres` (3 f32 a vertex): each vertex's centre of rotation,
worked out from the rest pose and the weights alone (`host::gui::centres`, `gui/skin-centres`, on
every core): the mean of the triangles' centroids weighted by area and by how alike their weights
are to the vertex's (Le & Hodgins' eq. 1 and 4, σ = 0.1), walked out breadth first over the
surface's POINTS (a UV seam's two sides welded: walked by vertex, each side found its own centre
and the seam opened as a dark crack) until the likeness falls under 10⁻⁶. A vertex on one joint is
its own centre. A game keeps the centres beside its model (b3d's `with-centres`, `PATH.centres`).

With centres, both painters skin a vertex on two joints or more by blending ROTATIONS: each
palette's joints are split once a frame into a unit quaternion and a stretch (polar
decomposition: `mesh::joint_rotations`; the GPU reads them laid after the palette's matrices, a
joint's at `start + joint count + joint`, so no new binding); the quaternions are summed
sign-aligned with the heaviest joint's and normalised (R), the stretches summed by weight (S̄), and
the vertex's matrix is `[R·S̄ | L·p* − R·S̄·p*]`: turned rigidly about its centre p*, which lands
where the blended matrices L put it. Everything after (morphs before, contacts, `:loose` with its
own palette's rotations, normals, shadows) is unchanged. Without centres, skinning is as before.
`gui/posed-positions` poses a mesh exactly as the CPU painter does, for probes.

Not done from the paper: its weight-space neighbour search seeding the walk (a vertex's alike
weights lie on the surface around its joint, which the walk reaches) and its subdivision of
triangles whose corners' weights differ by more than 0.1 (a body's triangles are small against
its weights' change).

**Consequences.** Measured on womanblend (each posed triangle's smaller stretch against rest, the
share squeezed under 0.6 across): arms up, armpit front 92% → 67%, under the collarbones 37% →
20%; the low mat squat's lower bottom 40% → 29%; folded arms unchanged (9% → 11%). Visibly
smoother at the armpit, no new fault. What remains is the method's own limit, vertices on three or
four joints with no one centre: the weights there are the next lever (fewer joints a vertex, or a
helper joint), the asset's. Cost: the centres once a model (seconds, cached); a frame, a quaternion
and polar split per joint and about one more skinning a vertex (timed in the GPU's scene pass).

## ADR-815 — The light under the skin takes a cast shadow, seen from just off the skin

**Status:** accepted, 2026-11-26.

**Context.** ADR-793 let a skin's scattered light near the terminator ignore its light's shadow,
because a closed body shadows itself there in the maps and would cut the wrap band off in a hard
line. The cost: a hand's or an arm's shadow on a grazing part of a face left the red wrap shining
inside it, a red edge to the shadow.

**Decision.** Near the terminator (facing under 0.3) the scattered part of a skin's light takes the
light's shadow looked up from the point lifted `SCATTER_LIFT × :scatter` (8 mm at `:scatter` 1)
along the surface's normal: clear of the body's own grazing self-shadow, inside a cast shadow's
reach; it fades to the ordinary shadow as the skin faces the light. Both painters; on the GPU only
where needed (a second lookup for skin within the band).

**Consequences.** A cast shadow darkens the wrap; the terminator keeps its soft band. On
womanblend the measured change is small (at most 8 levels of red): the red seen on her face from
¾ was her painted cheeks, not this.

## ADR-816 — A contact bounded to one side of a plane: `:within`

**Status:** accepted, 2026-11-26.

**Context.** womanblend lays the back of her thigh on her calf in a deep crouch with a plane through
the knee (ADR-805's `:only` on each side). An unbounded plane also flattened the skin over her

## ADR-817 — Expression wrinkles on skin: `:wrinkles`, and textures that are data

**Status:** accepted, 2026-11-26.

**Context.** womanblend's face needs the folds of an expression (crow's feet in a real smile, the
lines beside her nose, a frown) without shape keys for every fine crease: her plan
(`docs/research/face-wrinkles.md`) is Jimenez's two wrinkle normal maps and zone masks, each
zone weighted a frame by what her muscles do.

**Decision.** A `:skin` material may carry `:wrinkles {:compress h :stretch h :masks [h h]
:weights [8 numbers from -1 to 1]}`: two tangent-space normal maps on its UVs (skin pushed
together, skin drawn tight) and two RGBA masks marking eight zones (the first's red the first
zone … the second's alpha the eighth). At a texel each zone's mask times its weight is summed
apart for the compress map (weights above 0) and the stretch map (below), each map's tilt times
its sum, and the whole laid over the normal as the detail tile's is (`mesh::wrinkle_tilt`,
`detail_normal` at strength 1), on a mesh with tangents. Both painters; the GPU binds the four
textures at group 3, bindings 13–16 (8–12 are the contact passes' in the same module), the
weights ride in `DrawU::wrinkles`.

A mask's alpha is a zone, so these textures must not be filtered as pictures (premultiplied by
alpha, which zeroes a zone's red where its alpha is 0): a new texture filter, `:data`, stores a
texture as given, one level, read bilinear by both painters, every channel a raw number.

**Consequences.** A face's fine folds come and go with her expression, as a few texture reads on
skin fragments. The maps and masks are the asset's (baked in Blender); the weights the game's.
Wrinkle textures uploaded otherwise than `:data` read wrongly; `std/gui.blsp` says so.

**Amended 2026-11-26.** `:wrinkles` takes `:uv 1` to read its four textures by the mesh's second
UV set (`:uvs2`, glTF TEXCOORD_1; b3d-gltf reads it): a head's own layout, where on a whole
body's single layout her face was a tenth of the texture's width (1.5 mm a texel at 1024, too
coarse for a crow's foot). The second set rides in the vertex (26 floats) and through the CPU
painter's interpolation; both read wrinkle textures at their own size.

## ADR-818 — Contact shadows, and new scene features on the GPU painter only

**Status:** accepted, 2026-11-26.

**Context.** A shadow map cannot tell a shadow cast across a few millimetres: her lips over the
side teeth in a smile, a crease's wall, a finger on the next. Its texels and bias are coarser
than the gap, so the key light's highlight landed on teeth her lips covered, and they read as
chrome. And every scene feature so far was written twice, for the GPU painter and the CPU
painter, with tests holding them alike. That doubled each feature's work and its bugs (the CPU
copy's contact skip mask held 16 of 64 contacts), while the game is judged on the GPU.

**Decision.** (1) Each shadowed light's visibility is also multiplied by a *contact shadow*: a
march of 12 steps over 3 cm toward the light through the camera's depth view (the ambient
occlusion's, so a scene without `:ambient-occlusion` has none). A step that the depth view holds
something in front of, by more than a step and less than 1 cm (thicker is something apart in
front, not touching; 3 cm since her mouth), shades the point, the gap in front eased in from 1 to 3 mm, a near step fully,
one at the reach's end barely. It applies to the light's diffuse, its highlight and the skin's
scattered light alike. The same march along the REFLECTED direction (4 cm) dims the room's
reflection where the scene stands in its way: deep in her mouth her cheeks and lips hide the room
from her side teeth, which shone grey as chrome under a reflection only the ambient occlusion's
1.5 cm had dimmed. (2) The CPU
painter is brood's fallback (a machine whose GPU will not start, CI without one) and is
**frozen**: new scene features are written for the GPU painter only, and the CPU painter draws
the scene without them. Their tests check the GPU against expected values, not against the CPU.
Existing features keep their parity tests, and a bug in the CPU painter is still fixed.

**Amended 2026-11-26.** Neither march acts on a `:skin` surface. Light travels millimetres under
skin before it comes out, so its own creases never show shadows this sharp; marched on her neck and
collarbones they drew hard, aliased dark lines in every crease (the user). They act on what does
not scatter: teeth under the lips, eyes, nails, the room's things. A crease on skin is shaded by
its baked cavity and the scattering.

**Amended 2026-11-27.** The contact shadow acts for EVERY light, not only a shadowed one: a light
with no shadow map is as blind to millimetre occluders as one whose map is too coarse. Her fill
casts no shadow, and measured one light at a time it gave the side teeth behind her lips and
cheeks 36 % of all their light, the most of any source (womanblend-f9's teeth-colour probe, the
scream held). Still never on `:skin` (the tongue included), nor on hair.

## ADR-819 — A pressed mesh's normals from its posed surface

**Status:** withdrawn, 2026-11-26 (reverted, e958578f9). On a real figure the posed surface carries
the skinning's own creases, the straight edges where joints' weights meet (her collar and neck as
her arms came in across her chest): the posed face normals drew them as hard dark lines and
triangles that the carried normals had always hidden. Not shape keys, not the lagging palette,
not folded triangles (a trial skipping faces turned against their corners' carried normals left
them). It may come back once the weights are smooth across those edges and the correction is
held to where the surface truly bends. Was: accepted, 2026-11-26.

**Context.** A skinned vertex's normal was its rest normal turned by its bones (and keys), never
taken from the surface as posed. Where her skin bends hard over a coarse mesh (a squat's buttocks
and thighs, her armpit and shoulder with her arms up), the turned normals disagreed with the bent
triangles and the light lay in flat patches and pale streaks.

**Decision.** For a pressed draw (one the contact passes pose, ADR-805), two compute passes run
between posing and pressing: (C) each posed triangle's face normal, as long as twice its area,
is added (fixed point, `FACE_SUM_SCALE`) to its corners' welded points (vertices at one place
share a point, so a UV seam never splits the sum); (D) each vertex's normal becomes its carried
normal plus its point's posed face normal less the rest face normal turned as its normal was
turned. At rest, or moved rigidly, that difference is nothing and the authored normal stands (its
keys' normals too); only where the surface truly bends does the normal follow it. Its tangent is
squared to the new normal. The welds and rest face normals are made once at upload, for a mesh
with a softness. GPU only (ADR-818's rule); the device asks for the card's limit of storage
buffers a stage (ten in these passes).

**Consequences.** The light follows her bent skin; what a corrective shape keeps wrong now shows
truly (her arms-up shoulder ridge reads clearer) rather than half hidden by turned normals.

## ADR-820 — A normal map's handedness from the image's own way up, not the tangent's w

**Status:** accepted, 2026-11-26.

**Context.** A normal map's green is glTF's +Y, the image's up, and its bitangent is
cross(normal, tangent) × w. Her export's w was reversed on about two thirds of her skin (measured
against her UVs' direction of decreasing v), so every baked relief, pores, creases, her navel and
nipples, the wrinkle maps, was lit upside down: 67 showed her nipples reading cupped, raised with
only the green inverted.

**Decision.** The GPU painter takes the bitangent's sign from the surface's own direction of the
image's up at the fragment, from the screen derivatives of its world position and UVs (Schüler's
cotangent frame, the determinant's sign included), and keeps the stored tangent's direction; the
stored w only where the derivatives say nothing (a texel seen edge on). Green then always tilts
toward the image's up, whatever w a file carries, mirrored UVs included. The CPU painter is
frozen (ADR-818) and keeps the stored w.

**Consequences.** Her baked reliefs are lit the right way up; an asset whose tangents are wrong in
handedness reads right without a re-export. The detail tile and the wrinkle maps share the frame.

## ADR-821 — A hair lock's sub-steps natively, every lock in one call

**Status:** accepted, 2026-11-26.

**Context.** b3d-hair steps each lock of strand-borne hair (ADR-813) as a chain of points: a Verlet
step with a pull to the groom's shape, the roots' hold, a distance-constraint pass keeping each
bone off her body's shapes (capsules and panes) with friction, and the ground. In Brood that was
~140 µs a lock a sub-step, 26 locks run as 26 processes: about 5 ms of a 16.7 ms frame for the
hair alone, the user's "hair slowness".

**Decision.** `%hair-moved` (`host::hair`) takes every lock as b3d-hair's `moved-with` holds it
(points, last points, posed points before and now, pre-load forces, the shapes near each bone,
stiffness, damping, follows, holds, lengths, margins, bend limits, thickness) with the frame's
shapes, the number and length of sub-steps, and b3d-hair's settings as a map, and returns each
lock's points and last points. It is the same arithmetic as the Brood step, sum for sum in the same
order, so a lock comes out as Brood left it; the rules and their reasons stay documented in
b3d-hair, and no setting is fixed in Rust, so tuning the hair needs no rebuild. The per-frame setup
(the head's turn, the twist, which shapes are near) stays in Brood.

**Consequences.** The hair's step costs microseconds a lock and needs no process a lock. A change
to the step itself is made in both places, held together by b3d-hair's test against recorded
frames of the Brood step.

**Amended 2026-11-27 (b8 [1fa14e], with womanblend-39's profile): the whole frame's step.** With
the sub-steps native, the hair's frame still cost 7–8 ms turning round, two thirds of it each
lock's plan in Brood (`moved-plan`: the root untwisted from the head's turn, the curls' bounce,
the carried pre-load, the last step slid to this one's length, the shapes near each bone found
again past `*drift*`). `%hair-advance` takes the frame (every world matrix, the base, the twist, dt,
whether the colliders moved, the shapes) and every lock as the state keeps it, plans each as
`moved-plan` does (`host::hair::plan`), sub-steps it as `%hair-moved` does, and hands back each
lock's points, previous points, posed points, step, whether its near shapes were kept, the shapes
near it and its bounce. b3d-hair keeps its Brood plan as the reference (`stepped-locks-in-brood`)
and its test holds the native frame step to it exactly (apart 0.0 over 790 calls, the bounce on).
The hair's step turning round: 8.1–8.8 ms → 2.9–3.6 ms; `turned-lock` (0.5–0.8 ms) is the most
left in Brood.

**Amended 2026-11-27 (womanblend-39), stage 3: the links between locks.** With the plan, the
sub-steps and the joints' turning native (`%hair-turned`, stage 2), what b3d-hair's step still did
in Brood was holding the locks together as a mass and settling each lock the links moved (`cohered`,
`settled-again`): 0.8 and 2.7 ms a frame turning round (measured loaded, with the 22 × 7-bone
chains her hair now has). `%hair-linked` takes every lock (its points, old places, its bones'
lengths as its curls stretch them now, its margins, bend limits, hair thickness and near shapes),
the links (`links-of`), the frame's shapes and b3d-hair's settings, brings every link within its
bounds as `cohered` does (`host::hair::linked`), puts each lock it moved back to its lengths and off
her body as `settled-again` does (the sub-steps' `constrained_with`, its old places left as they
are), and hands back, a lock, nil where its points are as they were, else its points and old places.
b3d-hair keeps the Brood forms as the reference (`linked-locks-in-brood`) and its test holds the
native call to it exactly (apart 0.0 over 128 calls: the links moved a lock 214 times, 34 of them with shapes near its bones, and locks lay on the ground in 180, at 60 and
8 frames a second and lying on the ground).

## ADR-822 — Skin that slides: a relax where skinning squeezes it

**Status:** accepted, 2026-11-27.

**Context.** Skinning (linear, or rotations blended about centres, ADR-814) moves each vertex by
its joints alone, so where neighbouring vertices blend joints that turn very differently the skin
between them is squeezed and buckles: with her arms raised the skin of her shoulder tops is crushed
to half its rest length in the band nearest the arm and ripples in waves of 3–6 mm about 4 cm
apart, a pit 24 mm deep forms at the front of the collar, the skin under her collarbones nicks
when she folds her arms, and the front edge of the armpit creases (womanblend-67's measures on her
drawn mesh: four joints, clavicle, spine01, shoulder01 and upperarm01, share that skin in near
equal parts). Real skin slides over the body instead. Correctives (shape keys made for a pose)
patch one pose at a time; normals taken from the posed surface (ADR-819, withdrawn) showed these
creases the more plainly.

Published cures, read for this record:
- Delta Mush (Mancewicz et al. 2014; as restated by Le and Lewis 2019 and the Maya and Houdini
  manuals): smooth the posed mesh p times and add back each vertex's rest detail (rest minus
  rest-smoothed) in a local frame. Typical p 10–100; every frame; smooths everywhere unless
  painted; loses volume; frames degenerate where neighbours line up.
- Direct Delta Mush (Le and Lewis 2019): the same in closed form, a 4×4 matrix a vertex and bone
  precomputed (seconds at 56k vertices) and blended each frame; its baked rest data does not
  allow shape keys before it, and it relaxes everywhere.
- Implicit Skinning (Vaillant et al. 2013, 2014): fields a bone and a march back to the rest
  iso-value; contact and bulges, but about 15 fps at 32k vertices on its 2013 GPU and it rebuilds
  positions from fields. Its useful parts: smoothing only where a mask says (β, diffused), and a
  TANGENTIAL relax toward rest barycentric coordinates of the projected one-ring, which by
  construction does nothing at rest.
- Maya's Tension deformer: edge lengths restored toward rest with separate squash and stretch
  limits, moving only along the surface.

**Decision.** A pressed mesh (ADR-805, it is posed into the scene's buffer already) is relaxed
between its posing (pass A) and its pressing (pass B):
1. Its vertices are welded by place (a UV seam's copies are one point) and each point keeps its
   one-ring of neighbours (a CSR list built once at upload) and, at rest, where it sits against
   them in its own tangent frame: its offset from its neighbours' centroid, split into the part
   along its rest normal and the part across.
2. Each frame, each point's squeeze is measured: its most squeezed ring edge, posed against that
   edge at rest (each point's rest place is kept for it). A mean of the ring's edges stayed near
   whole where the skin buckles, squeezed one way and stretched the other, and the relax never
   acted. It acts by a mask rising from nothing at a squeeze of 0.95 to whole at 0.75 (smoothly,
   so nothing pops).
3. 48 Jacobi passes a frame (ping-pong buffers; each reaches a ring further, and the waves on her
   shoulder tops are about 4 cm apart on a mesh of 3–4 mm edges: 8 passes did nothing visible; of
   16, 48 and 96 filmed side by side, 48, c8's pick for the user): each masked point moves toward its neighbours'
   centroid plus its whole rest offset from them (along its normal and across), turned into the
   frame its carried normal and first neighbour make now: the masked Laplacian form of Delta
   Mush. A purely tangential relax evens the squeezed spacing but leaves the waves, which stand
   out along the normal; this flattens the buckle and puts the rest shape's own detail back
   there. At rest, or moved rigidly, nothing moves (the mask is nothing); skin not squeezed is
   not touched; where it acts, the keys' detail along the normal gives way to the rest shape's.
   The slide is capped (it carries the textures with it). The pressed dents follow (pass B).
4. Normals stay those its bones and keys carry, which never showed the buckle's waves; ADR-819's
   crease lines came from taking the posed surface's normals everywhere, over unrelaxed creases.

**Measured** (womanblend-67, the same relax applied to her drawn mesh after skinning, 2026-11-27):
the shoulder-top waves with her arms raised, in the band 10–16 cm out, fall from 13.9 mm at most
(rms 4.5) to 4.4 (rms 1.6); the lying armpit's from 16.7 to 10.8 mm; the ticks under her
collarbones with her arms folded ease (least edge 0.32 of rest to 0.50). The crushed length is
shared over more triangles, so more fall under 0.6 of rest: expected. The underarm side fold and
the armpit's front edge are left as they were: folds of the form, not buckling, for the model.

**Consequences.** Folds where skinning squeezes skin are slid out on the GPU in a few passes
(expected well under a millisecond at 66k vertices: each pass reads a point's ring once). Keys
that squeeze on purpose (correctives) are relaxed in part where they squeeze; correctives this
makes unneeded are dropped (womanblend-67), and a breast lift can ride the same stage. The CPU
painter is frozen (ADR-818): the relax is the GPU's alone, and the game's own probes, which skin
on the CPU, do not see it: a probe of the drawn surface reads it back from the GPU. Volume is not
restored by the relax: a raised arm may still need its shape keys. The relax acts only on squeezed
skin, never on stretched skin, so it never lifts a breast: the breast's lift and its move inward as
her arm rises are womanblend-61's, in her flesh layer, frame-exact from bones (the posed against
rest lengths from the breast joint to her sternum's front and to her armpit, pulling its anchor by
the stretched lines' resultant), with no read back from this stage.
## ADR-823 — How much of its draw's lag each vertex takes: `:loose-weight`

**Status:** accepted, 2026-11-26.

**Context.** A draw's `:loose` (ADR-811, ADR-812) draws its soft flesh toward a lagging palette by
each vertex's softness. Softness also says how a contact presses the skin (ADR-805), so it cannot
also say where the lag stops. Her nipple, drawn toward its own follower as a whole primitive,
moved its areola with it: the areola's outer edge parted from the skin round it and a dark arc
showed (womanblend's report, 2026-11-26). Only the nipple's tip should move, the areola staying
put.

**Decision.** A mesh may carry `:loose-weight`, one f32 a vertex in 0 … 1 (clamped), the share of
its draw's lag that vertex takes, multiplied into the share its softness gives; without one every
vertex takes all of it. It rides in the vertex buffer (27 floats a vertex now), read by the contact
passes, which are where the lag is drawn. The CPU painter is frozen (ADR-818) and ignores it.

**Consequences.** A primitive whose edge meets other skin can lag inside while its edge stays: her
nipple's tip bends from a still base (womanblend `womanblend-loose/nipple-weights`). Every vertex
costs four bytes more on the GPU.

## ADR-824 — How far short of a contact each vertex stops: `:standoff`

**Status:** accepted, 2026-11-27.

**Context.** Her skin pressed on a pane (ADR-805) goes right to the glass. Where her hair lies
between (her temple, the nape), the strands are clamped to the same plane (ADR-813's
`:clamp-planes`), so the skin covers them and the hair ends in a straight line at the edge of the
pressed skin, where it should read as a flattened mat seen through the glass (womanblend-39's
films, 2026-11-26).

**Decision.** A mesh may carry `:standoff`, one f32 a vertex in metres (0 … 0.05, clamped): the
contact passes measure that vertex's distance to every contact less its standoff, so it is pressed
to stop that far short of the surface, and its ring and drag follow from there. It rides in the
vertex buffer (28 floats a vertex now). The game gives it per vertex: womanblend the pressed
thickness of the hair lying over each point of her skin at rest (womanblend-hair/standoffs), zero
without hair.

**Consequences.** Where hair lies between her skin and a pane, her skin stops a hair's thickness
back and the clamped strands show between; elsewhere nothing changes. The CPU painter is frozen
(ADR-818) and ignores it.

## ADR-825 — A hair material's look: its scatter in shade, its spread, its tips

**Status:** accepted, 2026-11-27.

**Context.** Her red hair read crimson in shade and washed peach where lit, where real copper hair
is dark auburn in shade and copper lit (womanblend-39's photo patches: shade G/R 0.40 against ours
0.29, two and a third times too bright). No material setting cured it: the light scattered through
many fibres took its shaded colour as the strand's colour divided by its own luminance, which keeps
the shade as bright as the lit side and pushes red past red. The user also asked for the hair's
look to be set in the game, not built into the renderer ("cant this be a setting?").

**Decision.** In shade the scattered light has come through more hair: its colour is the strand's
raised to `:scatter-depth` (0.5) times the shaded share, darker and a little deeper in colour, not
renormalised (0.54 lit to 0.40 shaded in G/R is a power of about 1.5, the default's whole shade).
And a `:fibres` material carries its look as keys, today's values the defaults: `:scatter-strength`
(1), `:scatter-depth` (0.5), `:colour-spread` (0.2: each strand's lightness 1 ± it), `:tip-lighten`
(0: its last third lightened toward the tip, sun and wear), `:cuticle-tilt` (0.035 rad: where its
highlights sit), `:roughness-spread` (0.3) and `:absorption` (40 a metre, its self-shadow). They
ride in the draw's last two vec4s. Karis's fitted lobes, the 1.55 index and the shadow's sampling
stay built in: physics or the renderer's own.

**Consequences.** Shaded hair darkens toward auburn by default; the game tries looks with no
rebuild (womanblend's hair material, `WOMAN_MATERIALS`).

## ADR-826 — A skin's regions: its roughness, thickness, fine relief and oil by place

**Status:** accepted, 2026-11-27.

**Context.** Her skin was one material: one roughness, one scatter, one strength of the detail
tile, one coat, over her whole body. Real skin differs by place (womanblend docs/research/
skin-regional.md): oilier and shinier on the T-zone, chest and upper back, matte on the palms and
soles, drier on the shins; thin and translucent at the ears, nostrils and fingers, thick on the
back and soles; larger pores on the nose and cheeks, fine lines at the neck, wrists, knuckles,
knees and elbows (the user: "not uniformly the same color/tan and texture").

**Decision.** A `:skin` material may carry `:regions`, a texture in its UVs (womanblend-aa6d3b
bakes it beside the colour map): R its roughness (absolute, in place of the material's); G its
thickness, which scales the scatter (1 + (0.7 − G) × 1.5: the body's 0.7 is the material's own,
an ear's 0.15 near twice, a sole's 1.0 about half); B the detail tile's strength, normal and
cavity alike, stored over two (0.5 is the material's); A its oil, which scales the coat (0.3 is the
material's). One more texture in the material group (binding 5) and texture bit 2048; without a
map every value is the material's. The CPU painter is frozen (ADR-818) and ignores it.

**Consequences.** Her skin shines, glows and shows its relief by place from one baked map that the
model's pipeline owns; the material's own numbers stay the body's baseline.

## ADR-827 — The hair's opacity maps: hair shadows by its density, and her head shadows her hair

**Status:** accepted, 2026-11-27.

**Context.** The shadow maps hold depth alone, so a `:fibres` head of hair could only shadow as
a solid. Stamped whole, each strand drawn wide for the strands it stands for put a hard dark mark
on thin-hair skin; kept at random (`:shadow-keep`, Enderton et al. 2010) the marks thinned but each
kept one was still hard: blotches along her hairline. And a strand with a baked self-shadow
(ADR-813) took the bake in place of the shadow maps, and the bake counts hair alone: from behind,
the key in front of her lit the back of her hair through her head, and the hair's colour raised
to the many strands crossed made it a brighter, purer red (womanblend-39: twice the photograph's
shade, G/R 0.23 against 0.40).

**Decision.** Deep opacity maps (Yuksel and Keyser 2008). The depth maps hold what is opaque
only. Each shadow view has two more layers at `HAIR_OPACITY_SIDE` (1024): the hair's nearest
depth from the light, then its density, each fragment adding `-ln(1 - :shadow-keep)` to the
slices behind that depth it lies before (`HAIR_SLICES`: four, ending 2, 5, 10 and 25 cm past
it), so the light through n strands is `(1 - keep)^n`, the share `:shadow-keep` always meant.
A receiver reads the density at its distance (straight between slice ends), `exp(-density)`,
averaged over the light's penumbra there as `soft_shadow` does for an opaque blocker (the mean
nearest hair over 16 texels, the gap to the receiver times the light's size; 16 taps). Every
lit surface takes it; a strand takes it times the opaque maps a centimetre toward the light, and
a strand with a bake takes the bake times the opaque maps. Two passes a view, the hair's first
share of strands (`FIBRE_SHADOW_SHARE`) in each; two textures in the scene group (bindings 7, 8);
each view's `map.w` says whether it is a perspective view. A hair draw goes only into the views
its world box is in (`seen_by`: a cube light's faces mostly never see it), and each view says
where its hair can be in its maps (`hair_rect`, grown by the search): a receiver outside it
loads no texel. The CPU painter is frozen (ADR-818).

**Consequences.** Her head shadows her hair (the back's crown 131 32 14 → 85 32 14: only the red
the key sent through her head left), thin hair over skin gives an even faint shade, and
`:shadow-keep` keeps its meaning, now as a strength rather than a rate of stamps. Its cost on
the GPU, back to back on one glb (RTX 5060 laptop): drawn into every view, the shadow passes
4.1 → 8–15 ms; culled and bounded, no change measurable in them and 0–0.4 ms in the colour pass. Measured in
womanblend docs/film/opacity-b8/. What a fibre lets through on its way (`fibre_transmittance`)
is unchanged: her left side lit through her hair by the lamp stays a pure red, a question of the
hair's look rather than its shadow.

## ADR-828 — A fibre's absorption from its two melanins, not each channel apart

**Status:** accepted, 2026-11-27.

**Context.** The light a `:fibres` strand passes on (`fibre_transmittance`, ADR-813) took its
absorption from the hair's colour by Chiang et al.'s inversion (2016), each channel apart. For a
red hair (linear 0.38, 0.069, 0.0115 at roughness 0.65) that asks for absorption 0.038, 0.289,
0.806: green 7.6 times red, so red passes almost unabsorbed. No pigment does that: hair's colour
is two melanins, and pheomelanin, the red one, absorbs green 2.1 times as much as red. Her hair
lit through by the lamp beside her drew a pure, bright red (116 16 5), where red hair seen
against the light is a darker copper.

**Decision.** The absorption Chiang's inversion asks for is fitted by least squares as a sum of
eumelanin's (0.419, 0.697, 1.37) and pheomelanin's (0.187, 0.4, 1.05) absorption, d'Eon et al.
2011 as pbrt holds them, neither amount below none (`melanin_fit`). Her hair fits as pheomelanin
alone (0.747): absorption 0.140, 0.299, 0.784, green and blue as asked, red 3.7 times what each
channel apart gave. The hair's colour stays the one input; nothing is tuned.

**Consequences.** Light through her hair is copper, darker the more hair it crossed: from behind
her left side 116 16 5 → 63 15 5, the middle of her back 87 26 10 → 72 26 10 (a photograph of her
hair's shade: 60 24 8); the lit side seen from the front is unchanged. Measured in womanblend
docs/film/melanin-b8/.

## ADR-831 — Occluded ambient bounced back in the colour of the surroundings

**Status:** accepted, 2026-11-27 (womanblend-f9, agreed with womanblend-b8).

**Context.** A material's ambient is darkened by its occlusion (its `:occlusion` map times the
screen-space `:ambient-occlusion`), and what is taken away is simply lost: a deep place goes
grey-black whatever surrounds it. Inside a real mouth the shadowed teeth are lit by the light
bouncing off the gums and cheeks, so they go dim and warm (Velinov et al., "Appearance Capture and
Modeling of Human Teeth", ACM TOG 2018, §4.3); hers went grey-green.

**Decision.** A material key `:surroundings [r g b]`, the linear albedo of what surrounds the
surface. Where given, the occluded ambient is `bounced_occlusion(visibility, albedo)` per channel:
GTAO's multi-bounce fit to path-traced references (Jimenez, Wu, Pesce, Jarabo, SIGGRAPH 2016),
`max(v, ((a·v + b)·v + c)·v)` with `a = 2.0404ρ − 0.3324`, `b = −4.7951ρ + 0.6417`,
`c = 2.7552ρ + 0.6903`: never darker than the occlusion alone, given back mostly in the
surroundings' strongest channel. The ambient only: a light's own occlusion is its shadow
(ADR-793). It rides in `DrawU::sheen`, which only hair surfaces read for `:sheen` (ADR-799), so
the uniform does not grow. GPU painter only (the CPU painter is frozen, ADR-818).

**Consequences.** A material without the key is drawn exactly as before: GTAO's own default
(the surface's own colour) would brighten every fold of every surface, a look change of its own
to be judged apart. It only colours ambient that something already occludes: where the
screen-space occlusion cannot see a cavity (her mouth behind her lips, finer than its taps), the
depth must come as an `:occlusion` map.

## ADR-832 — A pixel-space quad may be rounded, anti-aliased and hollow

**Status:** accepted, 2026-11-27.

**Context.** A game's HUD draws its shapes as pixel-space `[:quad x y w h colour rot]`, and the
GPU painter filled every one plainly (`Edge::Plain`): no coverage at its edges. A round button
built of strips came out stair-stepped, a line at an angle (a turned quad, `gui/line`) aliased,
and womanblend's action wheel read as "blocky, badly rendered" to its user. The painter already had
the anti-aliased rounded box (`Edge::Rounded`: the signed distance to the box with a 1 px coverage
ramp), but only the cell grid's `FRect` reached it.

**Decision.** `[:quad x y w h colour rot radius outline]`, both appended and optional, so every
quad drawn before is unchanged. A `radius` (≥ 0) draws it through the rounded box, anti-aliased:
half its height a pill, half its side a disc, 0 a sharp-cornered quad with smooth edges (a crisp
line at an angle); no radius (nil, the default) the plain fill. An `outline` > 0 draws only the
band that wide inside the edge (a ring, a rim): its distance |d + w/2| − w/2, the same ramp on
both sides. The instance keeps its layout: the solid shader's `aa` slot, 0 or 1 until now, carries
the outline as 1 + its width. GPU painter only; the frozen CPU painter draws the plain quad
(ADR-818).

**Alternatives.** *A new op (`:shape`).* Rejected: one solid pixel primitive with two more
optional fields reads better than two that overlap, and no wire format carries ops. *An outline
attribute of its own in the instance.* Rejected for now: a new vertex attribute and pipeline
layout for a value the `aa` slot holds without ambiguity. *Circles from many thin quads.* What the
game did: aliased, and translucent ones double where strips overlap.

**Consequences.** HUD shapes are smooth at any size and angle; a disc's covered area matches πr²
within half a percent and the pixel its edge crosses is half covered (`gpu/tests.rs`, the shader's
coverage mirrored). Text in pixel space is untouched here: `:text-px` still draws in the cell
font at whole multiples of the cell size; a sized proportional face is a change of the text
layout and glyph cache of its own.

## ADR-833 — Pixel text at a pixel size, laid out by its font

**Status:** accepted, 2026-11-27.

**Context.** `[:text-px x y s face]` draws a game's labels, and it drew them as the cell grid draws
text: in the cell font (the bundled mono), a whole number of cells a cluster, at whole multiples
of the cell size (`:scale`). A HUD in a proportional face at the size it wants could not be had:
womanblend's action wheel read as a terminal's text pasted over a game ("why is it so blocky?").
The font system (cosmic-text's shaping, swash's rasteriser, the families `gui/font-register`
adds) and the GPU's glyph atlas were all there.

**Decision.** A `:px` in a text-px face (physical pixels, as the op's place is) draws the run at
that size in the face's family, laid out by the font's own advances: `Renderer::px_run` shapes the
whole run once (cosmic-text, advanced shaping), rasterises each glyph at that size, hinted as the
window says (ADR-730), and places it from the run's top left; the GPU painter uploads each glyph
once by its rasteriser key (font, glyph, size: `px_glyphs`) and draws it as the cell run draws a
glyph (the ground's contrast lift, ADR-611), the alignment by the run's real width, a `:bg` behind
it. Without `:px`, text-px is the cell run it was. GPU painter only; the frozen CPU painter draws
the cell run (ADR-818).

**Alternatives.** *A fractional `:scale` of the cell font.* Rejected: still monospace and still a
cell a cluster; a UI wants its own face. *Each cluster through the cell path's `build_cluster` in
a canvas of its own width.* Rejected: per-cluster shaping loses kerning and ligatures, and the
canvas is a cell's. *A run cache for pixel runs.* Deferred: shaping a label a frame is cheap next
to the frame (a few tens of glyphs), and the glyphs themselves are cached.

**Consequences.** Labels in Noto Sans (or any registered family) at 13 px read as a game's; a run's
width doubles with its size (`render.rs`, `px_run_tests`). Coordinates and sizes stay physical
pixels: a HiDPI game scales its `:px` as it scales its positions.

## ADR-835 — Each eye's own pupil: its size, place and outline, a crisp edge and a dark ruff

**Status:** accepted, 2026-11-27.

**Context.** An `:eye` surface (ADR-796) drew one circular pupil for every eye of its material: one
`:pupil` share of the iris, centred on the iris, faded over 8 % of its radius either side. A pair
of eyes so drawn are identical, which real ones never are ("both pupils are identical and that
is not possible"): pupils differ in size by about 0.2 mm, 5 % of a 4 mm pupil, and by 0.4 mm or
more in 19 % of people (Lam, Thompson and Corbett 1987); they sit about 0.2 mm toward the nose
and 0.1 mm up from the iris's middle (Yang, Thompson and Burns 2002); they are a few percent out
of round, each in its own way; and their edge is crisp, bordered by the pupillary ruff, the dark
scalloped frill of pigment epithelium 0.1–0.2 mm wide, never a soft grey halo.

**Decision.** Each entry of `:eyes` may say its own pupil: `:pupil-scale` (its size against the
material's `:pupil`, 0.5 … 1.5), `:pupil-offset [across up]` (its middle from the iris's, in iris
radii, u's way across), and `:pupil-waves [[turns depth phase] …]` (at most two: each adds
`depth · cos(turns · angle + phase)` to the outline's radius share). The material's `:pupil-ruff`
is the ruff's width in iris radii (default 0.025, 0.15 mm on a 5.9 mm iris radius; 0: none). The
GPU painter draws the edge half a pixel soft (the fragment's world footprint, taken in uniform
control flow, in iris radii; never under 1 % of the pupil's radius) and the ruff as a band of that
width beyond it, scalloped in 36 crenels phased by the eye, darkening the iris under it by 85 %.
An eye that says nothing has the material's pupil, round and centred. The pupils ride in
`DrawU::clamps` on an `:eye` (no eye keeps to clamp planes, no strand has pupils), so the draw
stays `DRAW_BYTES` 512. GPU painter only: the frozen CPU painter keeps its round, soft pupil
(ADR-818).

**Alternatives.** *A pupil texture each eye.* Rejected: the pupil changes size with light and
the shape is a few numbers. *Per-eye materials.* Rejected: one `:eye` draws a pair in one mesh
(ADR-796), and a second material for a second eye doubles its draw for a few floats. *Growing
DrawU to 768 bytes.* Rejected: every draw would pay for a field only eyes read. *The soft edge
kept.* Rejected: the 8 % fade reads as a grey halo at a portrait's distance; the eye's real edge is
sharp and dark.

**Consequences.** Two eyes of one material can differ as a person's do; the caller (womanblend's
`eyes-seen`) says how. The ruff and the crisp edge are on by default for every `:eye`.

## ADR-836 — A cloth's sheen: Charlie's lobe on a standard surface

**Status:** proposed, 2026-11-27 (womanblend-f9; womanblend-b8 [1fa14e] reviews).

**Context.** Her bikini is white swim jersey, drawn as a matte `:standard` surface with its knit's
relief as `:detail`. It read as smooth matte plastic: a knit's fibres throw a soft light back where
the cloth turns from the eye or the light (the sheen that tells cloth from paint and from skin), and
a standard surface has only its microfacet highlight, which a roughness of 0.92 spreads to nothing.

**Decision.** A material key `:sheen-cloth [r g b roughness]`, read on a standard, cutout or blended
surface: Charlie's sheen distribution (Estevez & Kulla, "Production Friendly Microfacet Sheen
BRDF", 2017), D = (2 + 1/α) sin(θh)^(1/α) / 2π with α = roughness², and Neubelt & Pettineo's
visibility, V = 1 / 4(n·l + n·v − n·l n·v), under each light's own shadow; the ambient's share by the
sheen's directional albedo E(roughness, n·v); and the base layer under it (diffuse and specular,
lights and ambient) scaled by 1 − max(colour)·E, so cloth with sheen is never lit brighter than the
light. E is integrated numerically and fitted for the knit range, roughness 0.3–1 and n·v 0.05–1,
within 0.05 of the integral (womanblend's `tools/sheen-albedo-fit.py`). It is glTF's
KHR_materials_sheen model (sheenColorFactor, sheenRoughnessFactor), so a pipeline can set it there.
It rides in `DrawU::strand`, which only hair surfaces read (their widths and highlight shifts):
DrawU does not grow. GPU painter only (ADR-818: the CPU painter is frozen). Units: this renderer
folds the BRDF's 1/π into the light (a matte surface is base · light · cos), so the per-light sheen
is π · D · V · cos; E is a dimensionless albedo and needs no π. Reading KHR_materials_sheen in
b3d-gltf is follow-up work: womanblend sets the key in its own fabric module for now.

**Consequences.** A knit, velvet or satin reads as cloth by its sheen at the edges it turns away
from the eye. A material without the key is drawn exactly as before. The sheen's reflection of the
room (the image-based part) is its albedo times the ambient's light, not a prefiltered environment:
enough for a matte knit, short of a satin's mirrored room.

## ADR-839 — The push-out net: a garment's vertices pushed out of her skin, every use counted

**Status:** accepted, 2026-11-27.

**Context.** A garment drawn on her own palette skins as her skin does (womanblend's clothing plan,
docs/research/clothing-pipeline.md), but where its fit or its weights are off, or a pose pulls
her flesh past what the garment's keys follow, a vertex of it can end up inside her: the skin
shows through the cloth. Games add a cheap last step for this, a garment's vertices found inside
the body pushed out along its normal (the plan's §4, "a safety net, not the fit"). It must never
hide a bad fit: every use is a fault to fix in the garment's build, so it has to be counted.

**Decision.** A contact may say `:garment true`: one of her skin's spheres and capsules (the
outfit's `skin_colliders`). It acts only on draws whose material says `:garment true` (and
`:deform`, so they take the contact passes): after the press, a last compute step
(`cs_garment_push_out`) moves each of their vertices found closer to a garment contact than
`GARMENT_MARGIN` (0.5 mm) out to it along the contact's own normal there (`contact_distance`,
eased by its `:within` side as every contact is), and counts it with an atomic add into a word
after the contacts' depths. The depth and press passes skip garment contacts, so her skin is
never pressed by them; her hands, the glass and the mat keep pressing what they pressed. Each
scene's count is copied after its contact pass into a read-back buffer, mapped after the frame
is submitted and logged a frame or two later (`scene_push_outs`, as ADR-801's GPU times), and
`gui/scene-push-outs` gives the recent frames' counts for the clip probe and the gate to report as
faults. The flag rides in the fourth float of the contact's drag vector and the material's as
texture bit 8192, so neither the contact list nor `DrawU` grows. The frozen CPU painter skips
garment contacts (it never presses her skin with them) and does not push garments (ADR-818).

**Alternatives.** *Garment contacts pressing through the press pass.* Rejected: that pass is
shaped for flesh (an eased band, a firm inset, a ring) and skips vertices without softness; a
garment wants its vertices put just outside, nothing more. *A signed-distance field of her body.*
Rejected for now: her skin's spheres and capsules are already fitted and posed every frame for
the hair and the ties; a field is the cure if the spheres prove too coarse. *Pushing in the
visual stage in Brood.* Rejected: thousands of garment vertices a frame in Brood, where the GPU
has them posed already.

**Her skin under the garment (amended the same day).** Pressed by a hand through a garment, the garment and her skin met the hand's capsule at the same radius (61 [10cd1d] measured the cup 3.7 mm inside the hand's reach). A mesh may carry a second standoff, `:cover` (one f32 a vertex, metres, 0 … 5 cm: the cover's thickness over each body vertex, the bikini's glb supplying it), held beside its softness in the press's storage buffer (two floats a vertex, so the vertex layout does not grow); a draw whose material says `:covered true` presses each vertex the larger of its ADR-824 standoff and its cover short of the contact, so the garment lies on the hand and her skin that much deeper. The flag is the draw's, so the outfit's toggle switches her cover on and off without uploading her mesh again (bit 2 of `DrawU.deform.z`, beside bit 1, has softness).

**Consequences.** A garment cannot show her skin through it where her spheres cover, and the
probes know how often that was needed: 0 is the aim, any count a fault in the build. Womanblend
passes her skin spheres as `:garment` contacts and marks the outfit's draws `:deform :garment`.
Cost: one more step in a pass that already runs, returning at once for any draw but a garment's,
and one word read back a frame.
## ADR-837 — What lies on skin shades it: the camera's view of contact casters

**Status:** proposed, 2026-11-27 (womanblend-f9; womanblend-b8 [1fa14e] reviews).

**Context.** Her bikini lies 1.5 mm off her skin, its rolled hems 3.2 mm round, and a real one
casts a thin shade on the skin along every edge. Hers cast none: measured across a cup's outer
edge, her skin ran on at the same brightness right beside it. A shadow map cannot resolve a gap of
millimetres (ADR-818), and the contact march that can was kept off skin entirely (ADR-818's
amendment of 2026-11-26), because marched against the camera's depth her own creases shaded
themselves in hard, aliased lines.

**Decision.** A material key `:contact-caster true`. When the scene has the ambient occlusion's
camera view and any caster, the shadow plan adds a second view, the same camera and size, into which
only casters are drawn (`ShadowView::casters_only`; its index rides in `SceneU::occlusion.w`). On
`:skin`, the contact march (ADR-818's: 12 steps over 3 cm toward the light) reads that view alone,
for shadowed and unshadowed lights alike. Cloth on her shades her; her own creases, never in that
view, still shade nothing. Every other surface keeps marching through the camera's own view. GPU
painter only: the frozen CPU painter skips the view.

**Consequences.** The shade under a hem is as fine as the camera's depth, and as cheap as one more
depth pass of the casters' triangles plus the march on skin pixels. Her hands could later be
casters on her own skin (fingers on a thigh) without bringing back the crease lines, provided a
hand is never a caster on itself: a caster view per body part, or the march skipping texels of the
receiving draw's own part; not built now. A material that is a caster but is not drawn (culled,
hidden) casts nothing, as it should.

**Measured** (womanblend, 2026-11-27, GPU stills of her bikini close and at 1.6 m from front, ¾,
side, above, below and behind, the same binary with and without the key: womanblend
`docs/film/bikini-fabric-f9-2026-11-27/edge-shade-*.webp`). Her skin within 2 px of the fabric,
against skin 11–25 px off: front of the cup 136 → 123 (−10 %), the cup from below −13 %, the
bottom from above −12 %, the bottom's front −6 %, the whole of her at 1.6 m −4 %; skin further off
unchanged; views lit from behind her barely change. **Open:** where fabric stands off her (the
halter strap at her neck, a cup's side seen from ¾ or from below), the shaded skin goes near black
(pixels under 35 % of the skin nearby: 103 → 1552 at ¾, 0 → 1165 from below). The march rightly
takes the direct light there, but nothing gives back what such skin really receives: the light
the white fabric bounces onto it, and her skin's own light carried under it. The cure is that
bounce, not a weaker march: the caster's albedo × the light it receives, sent back to the skin it
shades (a candidate: ADR-831's `:surroundings`, with the fabric in the surroundings).

**Amended the same day** (b8 [1fa14e]'s review; the bounce above was not the cause). Two changes
to the march. (1) A light with a size blocks in part: a blocker `h` in front of the ray `d` along
it covers h / (d · spread) of the light's disc, `spread` the tangent of half the angle the light
spans from the skin (`light_spread`; a point light keeps its one hard ray; the reflection's march
is never softened). Alone it changed nothing measurable, and neither did giving her fill and floor
bounce a size: the near-black was not penumbra. (2) What was: the march took everything in its
view as 3 cm thick, so from skin beside a hem seen edge-on, a ray toward the light that passed
BEHIND the hem, not through it, counted as blocked. The casters' view now has the cloth's own
depth, `CASTER_THICKNESS` 8 mm (hems 3.2 mm round, cups lined); the camera's view keeps 3 cm.
Measured with the frame taken at one moment of her clock (womanblend's `WOMAN_AT`, both halves
serial, so the pixel difference is the shade alone: no pixel lighter): near-black skin beside the
edges halved (cup ¾ 1719 → 932, from below 975 → 470; before the key, 111 and 0); skin beside the
edges still −7 to −13 % in the lit views, nothing further off. Sheets:
`docs/film/bikini-fabric-f9-2026-11-27/edge-shade-thin-*.webp`. **Cost** (GPU timer queries, a
busy machine): the colour pass at her bust 10.7–11.0 → 12.9–13.9 ms, the whole of her +0.6–1.3
ms: every skin pixel now marches 12 steps toward each of five lights, where before skin marched
none.

**The early exit: the casters' pyramid.** Probes along each light's way cannot skip the march
safely: a hem is crossed in its first millimetres, and two probes (the pixel's own texel and the
way's far end) dropped 16–94 % of the shade, five evenly spaced still up to 25 %. So after the
shadow passes a compute pass halves the casters' view level by level, each texel the nearest depth
of the 2×2 below (`CASTER_PYRAMID_SRC`; a level's last texel takes an odd row or column left
over). On skin, `caster_within_reach` reads the level whose texel spans twice the reach in the
view's pixels at the pixel's depth (a quarter more, and a pixel, for the march's rounding), its
2×2 texels nearest the pixel: a disc of the reach lies inside them
(`the_casters_pyramid_texels_read_cover_the_whole_reach`), and anything that blocks any light
within the reach lies in that disc. All four at the far plane: no march, for every light at once.
Measured against the full march, pixel for pixel in all 13 views: identical. Its cost: at her bust
the colour pass is still 12.8–13.6 ms against 10.8–11.4 without casters (three rounds each, on
figure B): at that framing nearly all the skin in view lies within two reaches of the bikini, so
little is skipped; the march itself (12 steps × 5 lights on skin near cloth) is what costs.
**Open:** that cost (see the review). **Known, kept:** a
dark line 2–4 px hugs a hem seen edge-on (the cup's inner edge from ¾): a real 1.5 mm gap seen at
that angle is dark, and the rest of its hardness is her fill and floor bounce being points, which
sizing those lights (the scene's) will soften.
